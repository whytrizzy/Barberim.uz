Barberim — choose a visual direction

Three directions taken from places everyone in Uzbekistan knows, plus one deliberately standard option for comparison. Each shows the client booking screen and the barber's "today" screen.

Only the look changes between them; the layout, buttons and flow stay familiar. All names, prices and times are demo content.

A · Metro My pick

From the Tashkent Metro: line colors, station markers, clean signage.

Idea
A barber's day is a metro line, and every free time is a station you can get off at. Each master has their own line color, so in a shop with three masters you instantly know whose calendar you're looking at.
Signature move
Free times drawn as stations on the master's line, and the barber's day as a vertical line with the current client as the station you're at now.
Palette
Cool marble grey, deep navy ink, and the metro's line colors for masters.
Type
Golos Text, a sign-like face built for Latin and Cyrillic.
Honest risk
Cities outside Tashkent have no metro. The metaphor has to stay quiet (line, stations, colors) and never turn into a costume with maps or tickets.

B · Tashkent Modernism

From Tashkent's modernist buildings: concrete sun-screen grilles (panjara) and the mosaic panels on them.

Idea
The schedule is a panjara grille: a calm concrete grid where every booked hour is filled with a mosaic color.
Signature move
Time slots and the barber's calendar as perforated grid cells. Booked cells take the master's mosaic color.
Palette
Concrete grey with mosaic cobalt, saffron, brick and teal.
Type
Unbounded (wide, architectural) for headings; Onest for everything else.
Honest risk
The wide heading face struggles with long Uzbek words on small phones. Grey concrete can feel cold to clients if photos don't warm it up.

C · Sartaroshxona

From the neighborhood barbershop found in every mahalla: mint tiled walls, chrome, the red enamel sign over the door.

Idea
The app feels like your own barbershop: tiled, clean, with the shop's name on a proper enamel sign.
Signature move
The shop's name as an enamel sign at the top. Times and bookings laid out as tiles on the wall, with the chosen one in sign red.
Palette
Mint tile, bottle-green ink, chrome grey, enamel red.
Type
Fira Sans Extra Condensed (sign lettering) for names; Onest for everything else.
Honest risk
It's the most literal reading of "barbershop" and can feel nostalgic or old-fashioned to young clients. The tile background must stay subtle on cheap screens.

D · Standard booking app for comparison

What most booking apps look like: white, one blue, rounded cards, system font.

Idea
Familiar and safe. Everyone instantly knows how to use it.
Honest risk
This is exactly the "generic app" look you said you don't want. It has nothing that says Uzbekistan or Barberim. It's here so you can see what the other three are moving away from.
